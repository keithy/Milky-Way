Every piece of artwork on the site is a component of its own. They are
listed here so they can be looked at, side by side, against the same
ground and in the same light.

## The banners

A banner is the artwork at the top of a page. The site has two kinds:
the gauntlets, who came first, and the balance, which came later and
brought the pans with it.

### The gauntlets

<Gauntlets alt="The Gauntlets, seated at a desk with a laptop" />

The gauntlets sit on a cloud that drifts. The cloud is drawn rather than
painted — a set of overlapping radial gradients — so it cannot show the
fringe that a cut-out leaves at its edges, and it takes its colour from
the theme rather than from the drawing.

### The balance, hanging level

<BalanceEven
    alt="A balance scale hanging level, with a Windows mark in one pan and a Linux mark in the other"
    icons={{ left: 'windows', right: 'linux' }}
/>

A balance with both pans at the same height. Neither pan is higher than
the other, so neither of the marks glows.

### The balance, tipping toward the left

<BalanceDownLeft
    alt="A balance scale tipping toward the left, with the Windows mark in the low pan and the Linux mark in the high pan"
    icons={{ left: 'windows', right: 'linux' }}
/>

The left pan is the low one and the right pan is the high one, so the
Linux mark on the right glows. The glow is the point: the rising side
is the side worth looking at.

### The balance, tipping toward the right

<BalanceDownRight
    alt="A balance scale tipping toward the right, with the Linux mark in the low pan and the Windows mark in the high pan"
    icons={{ left: 'windows', right: 'linux' }}
/>

The same artwork turned on its side. The scale is drawn once and
mirrored, because a mirror is cheaper than a second picture and the
drawing is near enough symmetrical that it does not show.

### The marks

The marks are named rather than given by path, so a post can say what
it wants in the scales without knowing where the artwork is kept.

<BalanceScale tilt="down-left" icons={{ left: 'windows' }} alt="A balance scale with a Windows mark in the low pan and the high pan empty" />

An icon on one side only, and the high pan left empty. The pans take
the marks by name, and either side may be left out.

### The cloud

The cloud under the scale is part of the scale's own component, not a
piece of its own. It is an ovoid, wider than it is tall, because it
stands in for the plinth of whatever sits on it, and it does not move.
A cloud that drifts is a cloud the reader watches rather than reads.

## The mascot

<Mascot alt="The Angelic Warrior, in flight" />

The mascot is a separate piece of artwork from the gauntlets in the
banner. He is flying, and the artwork came with its own transparency,
so unlike the seated gauntlets he needs no keying and no cloud behind him.

## The standing banner

<Standing alt="A figure with his arms folded, smiling, standing on a cloud" />

He stands with his arms folded, and he is narrower and taller than the
flying warrior — a standing figure rather than one spread out in the
air. Because he is tall, the banner gives him its height, and he stands
on the page rather than on a cloud.

## The rack

<Rack alt="A rack of servers, standing on a cloud" />

The whole rack, cut out of the background it was photographed against.
It is taller than it is wide, so the banner gives it its height and the
cloud fills the rest.

The artwork is a photograph rather than a drawing, so it needed no
keying. The rack is dark and the cloud is light, and the two need very
little between them.

## The warrior

<Warrior alt="A warrior standing with his arms out" />

A figure standing with his arms out. He stands on the page rather than on
a cloud, so the banner is left empty around him.

The artwork came as a premultiplied PNG, so the colour had to be divided
back out by its own alpha before the white backdrop could be keyed away.
That left a bright rim, which glows on the dark ground, so the rim is
pulled back to the colour of the ink.

### The warrior and the rack

<WarriorRack alt="A warrior standing beside a rack of servers" />

The same warrior, standing beside a rack. The two are taller than they
are wide, so the banner gives them their height.

The artwork came with a transparent background, so it needed no keying.
It did need the bright rim pulled back, the same as the rest.

## The halo

<HaloLink href="/works/" label="Offerings" />

A menu item whose first character carries a golden halo. The halo is a
ring set at an angle above the letter, as though the character were
catching the light. It fades in on hover and on focus.

## The registry

A banner is chosen by name, and a name that is not in the registry fails
the build rather than quietly falling back to the default. The same is
true of the marks in the pans.

<Banner
    name="balance-down-right"
    alt="A balance scale tipping toward the right"
    icons={{ left: 'windows', right: 'linux' }}
/>

That is the registry entry at work: the same component the works page
uses, named from the front matter of a post.
